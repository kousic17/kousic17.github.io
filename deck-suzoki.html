Project overview

The product:
Food customization app called "Suzoki" for a Japanese restaurant. Solves common ordering friction by allowing users to easily customize food orders based on personal ingredients, allergies, and dietary needs.

Project duration:
2-week design exercise to build a unique, trendy, and user-friendly mobile experience.

Google

Project overview

The problem:

My ideal customer is a man named Kodama (mid-20s, lives with friends in Japan). He wants to eat food of his own choice on a modest budget, but gets frustrated by long menus and hidden costs.

Needs: Quick customization, budget transparency, and minimal time spent ordering.

The goal:

Our food customization app lets users customize meal ingredients with upfront pricing. Measured by tracking user engagement and repeat orders placed within a week.

User research: summary

I conducted interviews with prospective users of food delivery apps and created empathy maps to understand user habits, pain points, and ordering preferences.

Going into research, I assumed users needing the app were primarily young working adults. However, research revealed that students and budget-conscious individuals felt an even greater need for quick, affordable meal customization.

Paper wireframes

Design Ideation

Goals & Thought Process:

I sketched multiple iterations of paper wireframes for the home and menu screens to explore quick ways for users to filter dishes. My primary focus was placing category pills (Sushi, Udon, Tofu, Tempura, Ramen) prominently at the top so Kodama could reach his preferred cuisine with a single tap, alongside a direct add-to-cart button on each food card to minimize navigation friction.

Usability study: findings

I conducted two rounds of usability testing with 5 participants to evaluate customization speed and pricing clarity.

Round 1 Findings:

  • Tested low-fidelity prototype with 2 initial participants to uncover structural blockers.
  • Identified navigation bottlenecks using an affinity map.
  • Price Ambiguity: Users hesitated at checkout because topping prices were not explicitly visible per item.

Round 2 Findings:

  • Fast Category Switching: Top navigation tabs reduced browsing time by 30%.
  • Cost Transparency: Adding (+¥100) tags next to each topping eliminated checkout surprise.
  • Sub-60s Ordering: Simplified steps allowed users to complete custom orders in under 60 seconds.

Accessibility considerations

1. Screen Reader Compatibility

Included descriptive alt-text for all food photos and maintained clear semantic heading hierarchy throughout.

2. High Color Contrast

Used WCAG AA compliant contrast ratios between text and buttons to ensure effortless legibility in bright sunlight.

Takeaways

Impact:

Testing a product with real users gives invaluable perspective. Usability studies proved that transparent pricing directly influences user conversion and reduces ordering anxiety.

What I learned:

The best way to validate usability is by testing early with diverse participants rather than relying on design assumptions.

Google

Next steps

1. Wider Usability Studies

Conduct another round of usability studies with a broader demographic to evaluate edge cases in delivery scheduling.

2. One-Tap Reordering

Introduce saved custom recipe presets so repeat diners like Kodama can reorder favorite bowls in a single tap.
